Import your history

Import your CSV from Fuelly, Fuelio or Spritmonitor — or any other CSV once you match its columns. Fill-ups you import go through the same full-tank rule as the ones you log, and records already in the car are skipped, so importing the same file twice adds nothing.

In Carsheet

  1. Save the file from your old app to Files, or send it to yourself by mail or AirDrop.
  2. Open Settings › Data › Import CSV and pick the file with Choose file…
  3. Check the preview: the number of rows found, the units Carsheet read from the file and the car the rows go to — one of your cars under Import into, or New car from this file. Change the units if the numbers look wrong.
  4. Tap Import. Rows that cannot be read are listed with their line number instead of being guessed.

Carsheet’s own CSV files (Settings › Data › Export CSV) come back the same way, with services and costs.

From Fuelly

  1. On fuelly.com, sign in and export the fuel-ups of your vehicle as a CSV file (Fuelly exports fuel-ups only; services stay behind).
  2. Import the file in Carsheet. Fuelly’s price is the price per gallon or litre; if your file holds the total paid there, switch on Price column is the total in the preview.
  3. A file with several vehicles asks which one to import; import the others one by one.

Partial and missed fill-ups keep their meaning: partial_fuelup becomes Partial, missed_fuelup becomes After a gap. Units follow the column names (miles or km, gallons or litres).

From Fuelio

  1. In Fuelio, export the vehicle’s data as a CSV file from its backup and export settings. Fuelio writes one vehicle per file.
  2. Import the file in Carsheet: the vehicle’s name, its distance and volume units and the date format come from the file’s vehicle section.
  3. Fuelio’s costs come along: a cost that names service work (service, oil, tyres, brakes, inspection) becomes a service, every other one an expense.

Full 1 is a full tank, anything else Partial; Missed 1 means a fill-up before it was not logged (After a gap). Price is the total paid, VolumePrice the price per litre or gallon.

From Spritmonitor

  1. On spritmonitor.de, export the fuel entries of your vehicle as a CSV file.
  2. Import the file in Carsheet. English and German column names are both read; the decimal comma is understood.
  3. If the file holds trip distances instead of odometer readings, enter the Starting odometer in the preview, and Carsheet adds the trips up from there.

The fill type keeps its meaning: full, partial, and the first fill after a missed one (After a gap). An empty type is a full tank.

From any other CSV

  1. Export your fill-ups from the other app or spreadsheet as a CSV file and choose it in Carsheet.
  2. Carsheet shows Match the columns with the first rows of the file. Pick the column for the date, the odometer (or the trip distance), the volume, and the price per unit or the total.
  3. If the file marks full tanks or missed fill-ups, pick those columns and the value that means yes. Without a full-tank column every fill-up counts as full.
  4. Tap Use these columns and check the preview as for any other file. The next file with the same columns is matched the same way.

Questions

How is fuel economy worked out, and why does a partial fill show no number?

Economy is measured only between two full tanks: the distance driven since the last full tank against all the fuel bought since then. A partial fill doesn’t show a number of its own — its fuel and its money are carried into the next full tank, which shows the economy for the whole stretch. Your average is the sum of the distances over the sum of the fuel, never an average of the single values, which would weight a short stretch as much as a long one.

What does “After a gap” mean?

Choose After a gap when you forgot to log a fill-up before this one. Carsheet can’t know how much fuel went in during the gap, so this fill-up starts a new chain instead of showing a wrong number; the next full tank after it shows economy again.

mpg (US) or mpg (UK)?

A US gallon is 3.785 litres, an imperial gallon 4.546 litres, so the same car does about 20 % more mpg in UK gallons. Carsheet always labels mpg with US or UK. Each car has its own distance unit, volume unit and economy display — miles, litres and mpg (UK) with prices in pence per litre is a normal British car — and changing them only changes how the numbers are shown: the history itself is kept in kilometres and litres.

How do reminders work, and what does “Unknown” mean?

A reminder can have a distance and a number of months, whichever comes first: “Due in 1,200 mi · or by 14 Feb 2027”. The binding one is the bigger text. To tell when the distance will be reached, Carsheet uses how far you drove over the last 90 days. With fewer than two odometer readings in that time, or none for 45 days, the distance can’t be forecast: the reminder shows Unknown next to its date and is notified by date only. Update odometer in the car’s header fixes it. Mark done logs the service and starts the interval again.

Why didn’t a reminder arrive?

Reminders are local notifications planned on your device; nothing goes through a server. Carsheet asks for permission the first time you save a reminder. Check that notifications for Carsheet are allowed (Settings › Notifications in Carsheet shows the state) and that a Focus doesn’t silence them. Carsheet plans the nearest reminders again after every entry and each time you open it, so open the app now and then.

Where are my backups, and how do I restore one?

Carsheet makes one automatic backup a day on your device and keeps the last ten; Back up now in Settings › Data › Backups makes one of your own, which stays until you delete it. A backup can be shared as a file. To restore, pick a version or Restore from file… and confirm: your data is replaced by the backup, and a copy of what was there first is kept in Backups. Backups hold your entries; receipt photos and PDFs stay in the app and in your device’s own backup.

How do I get my data out?

Export CSV in Settings › Data writes the car’s fill-ups, services and costs as CSV files for Numbers, Excel or Google Sheets — any day. Carsheet reads these files back, too.

What is in the Owner’s maintenance record?

The Record tab shows a PDF of the car: the table of services with dates, odometer and work done, the legal dates, a fuel summary, and the receipts attached after the table. Show prices adds or removes the cost column. Every page carries the line “Owner-maintained record. Entries are not verified by any garage, manufacturer or authority. Receipts attached where available.” The paper size is A4, or US Letter for cars in the US, Canada and Mexico.

Which widgets, Controls and Shortcuts are there?

On the Home Screen: Next Due (what your car needs next, by distance or date), Last Fill (your last tank’s economy against your average) and Two Cars (what each of your first two cars needs next); on the Lock Screen, the next due line. The Log fill-up Control opens the fill-up form from Control Center or the Lock Screen. In Shortcuts and Siri: Log a fill-up, Set odometer and What’s due.

Which devices and languages are supported?

Carsheet runs on iPhone and iPad with iOS 18 or later, in English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Brazilian Portuguese, Dutch, Polish and Russian. Distances, volumes, currency and dates follow each car and your region. It works fully offline.

How do I delete my data?

Delete single entries from the car’s History, a car with its records from the car editor, and backup versions in Settings › Data › Backups. Archiving a car takes it out of the garage and keeps its records. Deleting the app removes all of its data from your device. We hold no copy, because nothing is ever sent to us.
